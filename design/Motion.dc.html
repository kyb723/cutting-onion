Cutting Onions · Motion & interaction

How it moves

One idea runs through every animation: the page starts by making you cry and then refuses to let you. Motion is chunky and physical — squash, snap, hard shadows landing — never floaty fades.

1The onion field

Seventy-odd onions — each one the sunglasses mark off her glassware, drawn round with no legs and no lettering — drift under a soft pull toward the centre, piling into a heap that buries the icon. The cursor carries a repulsion field, so simply moving through parts the pile. Plain 2D canvas, hand-rolled solver — no physics library, no WebGL.

~74 bodies60fpszero dependencies
2Drag to cut

Move fast enough and the cursor stops pushing and starts cutting. Every onion the stroke sweeps through splits into two halves that fly apart along the blade, tumbling and showing their rings on the cut face — and each cut throws off a tear or two. The blade leaves a fading orange trail.

threshold 7px/framehalves + rings1–2 tears per cut
3The ticker never stops

A black strip of menu jokes rolls under the hero — PLEASE DON’T CRY · 唔好喊 · DEEP FRIED EVERYTHONG! · 曳野！ Hovering pauses it so people can actually read the line that made them stop. The footer runs the same strip in reverse.

34s per cyclepause on hoverreversed in footer
4Fourteen cuts in

The payoff. The field is made of onions already wearing the sunglasses; buried under them is the face still crying. Cut fourteen away, the icon stands clear, and the corner prompt changes from 拖曳，切啲洋蔥 to 而家好啲未？ You cut the onions, and nobody ends up crying.

at 14 cutsicon fully clearonce per visit
5Sections get cut open

Instead of fading in, each section is revealed by a thin orange line that sweeps the full width like a knife through an onion — then the content below slides up 24px behind it. Cheap to run, and it makes the whole page feel like one continuous chop.

wipe 340msrise 24pxfires once per section
6Dishes lean in

Hovering a dish card lifts it 7px, tilts it −0.7°, and snaps a hard black shadow in behind it. The price stamp counter-rotates back to level as it lifts, so the card feels like a physical thing being picked up off the pass.

7Menu tabs slide, list staggers

The orange pill physically travels from the old tab to the new one rather than blinking across, and the dish list underneath fades up in a 120ms stagger. On mobile the tab row scrolls horizontally and snaps.

8Language rolls, never fades

Hitting 中文 rolls each block of copy upward — English exits up, Chinese enters from below — staggered 20ms down the page so the change reads as a wave. Dish names never swap: both languages stay stacked, exactly like the printed menu.

Non-negotiables

  • prefers-reduced-motion kills every loop, wipe and roll. Content appears in place; nothing is lost.
  • Transform and opacity only. Nothing animates layout properties, so scrolling stays smooth on an old phone.
  • Nothing blocks reading. Every reveal has finished by the time a section is halfway up the screen.
  • Touch cuts too. A finger drag slices; the field takes fewer onions on a phone and never swallows a vertical scroll.
  • One payoff per page. Beat 4 is the moment — every other section stays quieter than the hero.
  • Lazy-load photography below the fold; the hero ships as the face mark plus type, so it paints instantly.
